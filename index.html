PrEP-ing at the end of HIV/AIDS

An anthropological study of Combination Prevention and PrEP in Argentina

Home / News About Publications Other Activities

The project investigates the implementation of Combination HIV Prevention, a comprehensive global strategy of the Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS) aimed at eliminating AIDS by 2030, and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) in Argentina.

The ethnographic research explores how global-local entanglements involving biotechnologies, institutions, policymakers, health professionals, activists, and PrEP users and their experiences come together in the making of Combination Prevention.

PrEP-ing at the end of HIV/AIDS is a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Postdoctoral Individual European Fellowship project, funded by the European Union under Horizon Europe (Grant Agreement No. 101200133). More about the project

    Being in Common: HIV Prevention, Belonging and Care in Buenos Aires

    Berlin Anthropology Seminars, 4:15–5:45 pm
    Freie Universität Berlin, Seminarzentrum, Otto-von-Simson-Str. 26, Room L116, and online

    Agostina Gagliolo will present her ethnographic research on PrEP in Buenos Aires at the Berlin Anthropology Seminars, a joint initiative of the Institute for Social and Cultural Anthropology at Freie Universität Berlin, the Leibniz-Zentrum Moderner Orient, and the Ethnologisches Museum.

    Since 2021, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), an antiretroviral medication used to prevent HIV transmission, has been available through Argentina's public health system as part of the broader strategy known as Combination Prevention. Within this increasingly biomedical prevention framework, PrEP has largely been approached as a tool for individual risk management. Recent literature, however, shows that practices around it often exceed individual responsibility, extending into solidarity and care for others.

    Drawing on ethnographic fieldwork at an LGBTQ+ civil association and Combination Prevention Center in Buenos Aires, this talk approaches the circulation of this pharmaceutical as it takes part in the enactment of what a group of self-identified gay men describe as a collectively inhabited environment. Along the way, PrEP becomes laden with affect, enabling some relations and interrupting others, and thereby transforming what is conceived as possibilities of belonging.
